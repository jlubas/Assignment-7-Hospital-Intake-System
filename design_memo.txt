Why is a tree appropriate for the doctor structure?

I think a binary tree is a good choice for organizing the doctor reporting structure because it clearly shows the relationships between doctors.
Each doctor has a specific position in the tree, and the root represents the doctor at the top of the hierarchy.
For example, in our assignment, Dr. Croft is the root, while Dr. Phan and Dr. Goldsmith report directly to Dr. Croft. 
I think this structure makes it easier to understand how the hospital's reporting system is organized.

When might a software engineer use preorder, inorder, or postorder traversals?

While working on this assignment, I found that different traversal methods are useful for different tasks.
Preorder visits the root before its children, which is helpful when copying a tree. 
Inorder visits the left subtree, root, and right subtree, producing sorted values in a binary search tree.
Postorder visits children before their parent, making it useful when deleting a tree. Each traversal method has a different purpose depending on the task.

How do heaps help simulate real-time systems like emergency intake?

I think a min-heap is useful for emergency intake because patients are prioritized by urgency instead of arrival order. 
In my program, urgency scores range from 1 to 10, with 1 being the highest priority. 
For example, Taylor has an urgency of 1 and should be treated before Jordan, whose urgency is 3.
When a patient is added, heapify_up() moves them into the correct position. After removing the most urgent patient using remove_min(), heapify_down() restores the heap property. 
A heap helps me find the most urgent patient quickly without having to check every patient one by one.
